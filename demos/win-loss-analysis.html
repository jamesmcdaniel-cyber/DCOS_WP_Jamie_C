Sales Outreach Themes (People.ai)

✓ NB Won — What Made Them Close

Cisco ($1.4M) — Technical Champion + Multi-Stakeholder
Now managing PeopleGlass login/RBAC issues across multiple internal teams. Win was driven by broad internal adoption and a strong technical champion who escalated blockers to engineering.
NVIDIA ($774K) — Proof of Concept → Pilot
Sales.ai POC successfully transitioned to pilot phase. Key hurdles overcome: data privacy concerns, PowerBI preference, complex filtering needs. Executive sponsorship drove past technical objections.
Visa ($198K) — Integration-Led Motion
Win was tied to successfully completing MS Dynamics + Outlook integration. Rep coordinated closely on RBAC/O365 access. Deal stalled until integration was unblocked — then moved to SalesAI enablement.
TransUnion ($234K) — CS-Led Expansion
Renewal dashboard built for senior leadership. Launch of 280 licenses across 7 regions in first week of August. Value proven through a CRO dashboard — deal expanded from CS relationship.
Dataiku ($300K) — Long Cycle, Integration Focus
Deal involved complex Salesforce field additions and OCR corrections. Multiple technical validation loops. Rep navigated data integrity concerns and pricing misalignment before close.

✗ NB Lost — Why They Didn't Close

Dell ($750K) — Leadership / Timing
Deal stalled on leadership transitions. Rep engaged partnership angle but deal awaits new CRO onboarding. Annual sales conference caused delay. No champion identified who could advance the deal.
Oracle ($500K) — No Urgency
Outreach initiated but no traction. Rep exploring collaboration opportunities with multiple contacts. Timing was described as "not right." No defined use case or internal sponsor driving purchase.
IBM ($500K) — CEO Directive Pause
Project paused due to direct CEO directive. Security/O365 access concerns raised. Deal had active implementation discussion but executive blocker terminated the cycle.
Xerox ($528K) — Demo Stage Only
Outreach at early demo stage — rep presenting forecasting tool value. No technical evaluation or stakeholder expansion visible. Deal never advanced beyond initial intro.
Cloud Software Group ($691K) — Decided Not to Renew
Formal non-renewal letter sent. Contract termination acknowledged by account team. Despite active onboarding discussions, the customer did not see sufficient value to continue.
Won Pattern #1
Technical Champion Present
Every Strategic win had an internal champion escalating blockers, driving integration, and coordinating across teams. Cisco, NVIDIA, Visa, TransUnion all show this pattern.
Won Pattern #2
Integration + POC → Expand
Wins followed a structured technical evaluation — POC to pilot to full deployment. Web Content engagement was 28% of all touches on won accounts, showing self-serve research behavior.
Lost Pattern
No Champion / Timing Blockers
Lost deals share three themes: no identified internal sponsor, executive-level blockers (CEO directive, leadership changes), or deals stuck at demo stage with no evaluation progress.

Strategy Scorecard

Strategic Assumption Verdict Evidence Limitation / Watch
Prioritize Strategic NB ⚠ Mixed Median won ACV $111K — highest of all segments. Win rates (16%) are low but deal size justifies effort if pipeline volume supports it. Win rate is the lowest. Every 6 Strategic deals in pipeline yields ~1 win. High effort-per-win. Champion identification is the gating factor.
Prioritize Large Enterprise NB ⚠ Mixed Median won ACV $78K. 13% win rate — lowest of any segment. Cycle time shows no difference between won and lost (p=0.95). Weakest win rate of all segments. No timing signal to predict outcome. High marketing spend with lowest conversion observed.
Small Enterprise NB opportunity ✓ Supported 27% win rate at $66K median ACV — strong combination of conversion and deal size relative to effort required. Smaller total pipeline volume. Ensure sufficient Stage 2 pipeline is being built in this segment.
Minimal focus Velocity NB ⚠ Revisit 32% win rate — highest of Strategic/LE/SE/Velocity tiers. Deals close in 32 days (vs 91 lost). Lower ACV ($26K) but faster and more efficient to close. ACV is low. If rep capacity is constrained, Velocity NB is efficient but not high-value. Could be a good fit for a lower-touch sales motion.
Inbound-only Small Business NB ✓ Supported 65.8% win rate, 20-day cycle. Respond when they arrive — data fully supports this. Not worth outbound investment given $15K median ACV. SB win rate is artificially high — small sample, high inbound intent self-selection. Don't build outbound programs here.
Focus upsell on Strategic & Large Enterprise ✓ Supported Strategic median won ACV $94K vs SB $2.4K. Win rates are equal across all segments (p=0.20). Prioritization is ACV-driven, not win-probability-driven. Concentration risk — Strategic upsell losses are growing faster. Upsell deals past 4–5 months (Stage 2 to close) rarely recover.
Marketing: quality over volume ✓ Supported Won accounts averaged 48 touches vs 208 for lost. Won accounts had 28% Web Content share vs 3% for lost. High-intent engagement (web content, drip) is associated with better outcomes. Selection bias: web content engagement may reflect pre-existing intent rather than marketing causing conversion. Directionally sound but not causal.

Qualified pipeline = Stage 1 date recorded AND Stage 2 date recorded in SFDC. 496 NB Closed Lost deals without a Stage 2 date excluded. Sales cycle measured Stage 2 date → Close Date. Statistical tests: chi-square for win rate differences; Mann-Whitney U (two-sided) for cycle time. ACV = median of Closed Won deals.
